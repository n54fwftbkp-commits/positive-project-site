# Methodology

Positive Project entries begin with a deliberately simple proposition, usually that a subject “is not a bad thing.” The proposition is then interpreted in the strongest reasonable form rather than as an absolute claim that every instance or consequence is good.

## Working method

1. Define the subject and separate it from related but distinct harms.
2. Identify genuine benefits, constructive functions, neutral qualities, or burdens the subject may prevent.
3. Avoid depending entirely on criticism of an opposing view.
4. State material qualifications and boundaries plainly.
5. End with a concise position that preserves both the positive case and the relevant limits.

The project favors charitable interpretation, proportional judgment, precise distinctions, and language that remains understandable outside specialist settings.

## Revisions

Published entries are not treated as infallible. Corrections, counterarguments, better sources, and clearer formulations may justify revision. Substantive changes should preserve the history of the public repository.
